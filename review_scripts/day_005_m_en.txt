A reel flashes, "Thirty percent off a bike." You almost send it to a friend. Stop on the word "off". Off what price, for which model, in which city, and for whom?

Imagine the listed bike price is one lakh rupees. Thirty percent of that is thirty thousand rupees. That is only the arithmetic of the headline, not proof that your bill falls by thirty thousand. Maybe the offer has an eligibility rule, a different variant, a trade-in condition, or an expiry. We do not know from the caption.

Ask the seller for a written, itemized on-road quote. Compare the bike price, tax, registration, insurance, and any accessories. Ask them to show the discount as a separate line. Check the offer terms and the final amount you would pay, including finance costs if you borrow. Compare like-for-like quotes before paying any deposit.

This does not call the reel or a dealer a scam. It turns one exciting percentage into a question you can check. Follow Market Debunk, and share this with a friend about to buy a bike.
