A friend sends you a chart. "This stock went up five percent. I could make money trading it." The chart looks easy because it has no receipt attached.

Suppose you put ten thousand rupees into a position and sell after a one-percent price rise. That's one hundred rupees gross, before brokerage, taxes and other charges. Slippage may move your execution price too. If costs total, say, forty rupees in this illustration, the net is sixty, not one hundred. The forty is just an example; check your broker's actual charges.

A one-percent move against you means a hundred-rupee price loss before costs. A chart does not tell you which direction comes next, and repeated trading can make small charges add up.

If your goal is long-term investing, start with your time horizon, risk capacity and a product you understand. Do not copy a trading screenshot and call it a plan.
